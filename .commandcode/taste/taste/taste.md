# Taste
- Wants maximal, immersive UI: abundant animations, rich styling/shine effects, "feature-bombed" pages — plain or sparse designs are rejected as "too less". Explicitly wants a look "no one has ever created" — unprecedented/original designs, not conventional or off-the-shelf UI. Confidence: 0.9
- Prefers a single strong immersive landing page whose visual language then spreads across the whole site. Confidence: 0.7
- Likes gamified/themed experiences (game and movie themes) that users emotionally relate to, not exact copies but an original take on them. Confidence: 0.6
- Prefers multi-page sites with multiple templates and layouts over a single-page site; expects all pages to be brought up to the same redesigned quality, not just one — states it as "every single page". Confidence: 0.8
- Expects both sides of a personal site to be fully built out: the admin/internal panel used as his own operating tool, plus a separate public/non-admin experience. Not one at the expense of the other. Confidence: 0.6
- Wants real, verifiable content instead of placeholder or "bad" data — e.g. actual public repos listed with proper descriptions, data seeded from real proof, certificates/timeline pulled from real source material. Confidence: 0.9
- Zero tolerance for fabricated credentials: invented roles, clubs, papers, awards, or metrics (even ones that make the portfolio look better or fuller) destroy the site's value — "that makes its values near 0". Only assert what is in his resume/certificates or what he has explicitly told you; when unsure, ask or omit, never infer and present it as fact. Confidence: 0.9
- Wants the full breadth of what he actually provided surfaced — every internship, participation, award, and ongoing research — not a curated subset. Omitting material he handed over is treated as the work not reflecting him at all ("nothing reflects", "a joke to me"). Read his source files (`classified/`, resume PDFs, certificates) and mine them before writing page content. Confidence: 0.8
- Wants unwanted/stale content and database records cleaned out rather than left in place. Confidence: 0.6
- Prefers creating a new branch before attempting experimental or risky rework. Confidence: 0.7
- Prefers free / free-tier tooling and cost avoidance — e.g. free Groq Whisper for transcription and rotating multiple Gemini API keys to stay within free limits rather than paying. Any integration should default to a free service and only escalate on approval. Confidence: 0.7
- Wants multiple LLM providers wired in as real, first-class options (e.g. Groq alongside Gemini) rather than a single hardcoded one, and he will hand over keys to make that happen. The model chosen must be capable and durable — something with real value that won't be deprecated/disappear "in a moment" — not a throwaway preview model. Confidence: 0.65
- Prefers varied, multi-accent color palettes over a single-hue scheme — a one-colour (e.g. all-lime/all-green) theme reads as unfinished; add secondary/tertiary accents across headings, buttons, and backgrounds. Confidence: 0.65
- Rejects internal jargon as user-facing copy: labels like "scenes" / "marks" that only make sense from the source code must be renamed to plain, self-explanatory words ("repos", "featured", "awards"). Confidence: 0.65
- Prefers short, trimmed surfaces over exhaustive dumps — cap long card lists and drop low-value entries he never uses, while keeping the full archive reachable behind a separate action. Confidence: 0.6
- Wants automation to execute end-to-end but never unsupervised on external actions: an approval queue for anything outward-facing or destructive, explicit safety/ethics guardrails, and an audit log of every action. Escalates this to a hard requirement: the gate must be enforced in code and must not be bypassable — "do not post unless I say something or press", because autonomous actions can be harmful — with an emergency stop. Treats these guardrails as the first thing to get right, above adding more features. Confidence: 0.85
- Expects gaps stated plainly: if part of what he asked for was skipped or is not built yet, say so explicitly and name what's missing instead of presenting the subset as complete. Confidence: 0.6
- Cares about output/asset quality — wants the best-quality version of visual assets (e.g. full resolution, no downscaling, minimal compression, "best quality") and likes to compare several candidate options (contact sheet, numbered choices) before picking the final one. Confidence: 0.7
- Judges image crops/photos by whether the subject (himself) is clearly and flatteringly in frame — a crop that zooms past him is rejected; keep him prominent while framing the subject he asked for. Confidence: 0.6
- Prefers non-destructive local dev: when a port is already taken by another of his projects, start the app on a different free port instead of stopping/killing the other project's server. Confidence: 0.55
- Expects git state to be reported honestly: whether the work is committed, how far the branch is ahead/behind `main`, and whether it can merge/push cleanly — while leaving the actual commit/push for him to run. Confidence: 0.65
- For a portfolio, rejects minimalism outright — "simplicity is not made for portfolio" — and wants a much heavier, more elaborate design, but still legible: heavy is fine, confusing is not. Confidence: 0.75
- Wants a genuinely end-to-end integrated platform, not isolated features: real external integrations across his own accounts (LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, X, Reddit, GitHub, n8n or similar) plus contact, publishing, and automation wired together inside one app. Confidence: 0.7
- Judges features by whether they save him future time — build the automation once so recurring daily chores stop being manual repetition, rather than spending days on them by hand. Confidence: 0.6
- Prefers being asked direct questions to fill gaps in his own information instead of the assistant guessing, assuming, or silently omitting what it can't find. Confidence: 0.6
- Wants the portfolio to position him as a company founder/professional, not merely a student — founder status and the company should be foregrounded, not buried as a side note. Confidence: 0.65
- Wants credentials and research stated at their true stage: papers he has read must not be presented as papers he wrote, and his first in-progress paper should be labelled as such. Confidence: 0.75
- Judges the portfolio's credibility by whether a stranger would accept it as a real person's record; AI-generated look is forgivable, but content that would fall apart under scrutiny ("fake values") is not. Prefer fewer, thinner, fully defensible entries over richer invented ones. Confidence: 0.75
- Expects key entry points to be discoverable from the public site itself — e.g. an obvious login link rather than having to be told the URL. Confidence: 0.5
- Treats unreadable text as a bug: when contrast/palette choices render UI text invisible or near-invisible, that dominates the complaint and must be fixed with real contrast (brighten muted tokens, never leave `color: transparent` without a solid fallback). Confidence: 0.75
- Reads small type and small-scale layout as dated ("small, boring, old-age"); wants large, generous type scale and a modern feel rather than tiny, cramped elements. Confidence: 0.65
- Wants the agent to state its plan and what it did in the chat message itself, not only in tool calls — "tell me in chat as well, I will read and sleep" — so he can review the work later without watching it happen. Confidence: 0.7
- Prefers reusing and integrating existing open-source code/repos over writing everything from scratch, to fill features faster. Confidence: 0.6
- Benchmarks his assistant against commercial products (Siri, Google Assistant, mainstream chatbots) and wants it to clearly outperform them — ambitious capability bar, not "good enough for a demo". Confidence: 0.75
- Wants capability breadth consolidated in his own app rather than split across tools: explicitly resists building features "apart" or settling for a subset — every relevant capability should land in the one app. Confidence: 0.7
- Expects background automation (workers, job queues) to actually run reliably — retries, persistence, a health/status check — rather than a half-wired worker that silently does nothing. Confidence: 0.6
- Wants a large batch of pending work pushed through in one session rather than a small increment ("do as much as you wanted to fill", "ok do the rest now"); measures the session by how much unfinished work got closed out. When the agent proposes picking up one single next item, his answer is to clear the whole remaining list — not that one item. Confidence: 0.7
- Judges a design pass by whether the visual system visibly changed — recoloring/contrast tweaks on the same layout still reads as "the same old UI" and is treated as no progress. Confidence: 0.6
- Requires status/config surfaces to tell the truth: an integrations page that shows a red ✗ / "key not configured" when the key IS configured, or that keeps displaying a stale error string from a since-fixed problem, is read as the whole app being fake or lying. Every configured provider must be listed with its live state, and resolved errors must stop being shown. Confidence: 0.7
- Judges "done" by the actual user-facing app behaving correctly, not by API/curl/backend test output — server-side 200s and log checks do not count as proof when the screen still looks wrong. Confidence: 0.65
- Wants to be told plainly when the current model or tooling is the wrong fit for a task, so he can switch models/tools himself, rather than having it silently produce weaker results. Confidence: 0.55
- When a large feature set is requested, wants a complete written feature inventory first (what ships, what's partial, what's missing), then the build to start immediately from that list in the same session — not a plan left as a plan. Confidence: 0.65
- Delegates the build-vs-buy / tooling decision to the agent ("n8n or an alternative, that's on you") and expects it to choose and execute rather than ask for permission or stall. Confidence: 0.6
- Explicitly prefers iterative, phased execution over big-bang delivery: leftover plan items should be rolled into the next phases and worked through continuously, one pass at a time — "iteratively doing is much better than any other work here always". Do not stop to ask; just take the next slice. Confidence: 0.75
- Treats mobile as a first-class surface, not an afterthought: expects a dedicated, genuinely better mobile UI (bottom nav/dock, single-column stacking, touch-friendly) rather than a shrunken desktop layout. Confidence: 0.7
- Wants his personal communication channels (WhatsApp, SMS, calls) controllable from inside the app so he does not have to operate those apps manually — convenience/automation over a chat client. Confidence: 0.7
- Grants long working sessions and judges them by quality ("I do give you a lot of time do things like this better") — do not trim scope or rush to finish for time's sake. Confidence: 0.6
